## Published examples and what was verified

**ENGINE — A scalable equivariant graph network framework for precise protein function prediction (Genome Biology, 2025).** Official [article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03886-y), [Figure 1 page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03886-y/figures/1), [publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13059-025-03886-y.pdf). The article text and Figure 1 caption were read; Figure 1 occurs on PDF page 4 (zero-based page index 3). The caption separates data collection from architecture, and explains the architecture through complementary structural, 3Di sequence and sequence channels that converge on feature fusion and classification. The applicable organizational lesson is to make complementary inputs and their merge identifiable at a glance, with distinct upstream representations and one unambiguous downstream output. This is an inference from the caption and method description, not a visual assessment of the image.

**OPUS-PLLM — Advancing generative large language models toward discriminative performance in protein function prediction (Genome Biology, 2026).** Official [article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-026-04109-8), [Figure 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-026-04109-8/figures/1). The complete Figure 1 page and its caption were read, together with the relevant overview. Here Figure 1 compares two prediction workflows, rather than exposing every internal layer. The model overview describes modality encoding, modality refinement and instruction tuning as three coherent steps. For FlexPPI this supports showing the full static-assembly-to-dynamics question once in panel a, then using subordinate panels to explain the distinctive geometry and supervision. It does not justify copying this paper's exact composition or visual style, which was not visible through the available tools.

**SpatPPI — A geometric deep learning model for predicting protein–protein interactions involving intrinsically disordered regions (Genome Biology, 2025).** Official [article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03820-2), [architecture Figure 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03820-2/figures/2), [publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13059-025-03820-2.pdf). The article, architecture caption and local-coordinate-frame description were read; architecture Figure 2 occurs on PDF page 5 (zero-based index 4). This paper's Figure 1 is biological motivation and its model architecture is Figure 2, so it must not be cited as a Figure 1 architecture example. Its architecture caption first distinguishes embedding generation from residue-contact capture, and describes local geometry within the embedding stage. The useful inference is to give geometry a defined role in the main pipeline and reserve the geometric explanation for a separate, legible detail, rather than drawing all representations at equal visual prominence.

## Findings from the visible current FlexPPI figure

The main left-to-right story is already useful: one assembly produces residue/pair/geometry representations, a shared encoder processes them, a joint head predicts two dynamic quantities, and MD provides supervision. The principal weakness is the visual vocabulary inside that structure. Numerous large bold headings, similar-sized colored boxes, multiple border and connector styles, and densely stacked encoder/head layers compete for attention. These choices make implementation details appear as important as the biological input and outputs. Repeating the same word-size hierarchy in panel a and the three lower panels adds visual noise.

Panel b is the clearest problem. The whole pale-blue protein, a large global XYZ system and origin, three small local coordinate systems, three residue-name labels, a translation vector and a transformation formula all overlap or compete within one small field. The three residue names do not advance the geometric explanation. The image has many geometric marks but does not establish a single reading sequence from backbone atoms to a local frame to the point comparison used by attention. Simply making the protein render sharper would preserve this compositional problem.

## Executable redesign recommendations

Preserve the overall architecture and scientifically verified operations. Rebuild the internal elements with a small, consistent set of symbols: a ribbon for the biological assembly; circles joined by short bonds for backbone atoms or residue nodes; a compact strip for residue features; a small square grid for pair features; a small triad for a local frame; restrained modules for learned transformations; and a clean pair of output tracks for RMSF and Hχ. Use three feature colors consistently across panels—blue for scalar/residue information, muted ochre for pair information, violet for geometric points—and one restrained green only for MD supervision. Colors should identify information, not decorate every box. The predicted RMSF structural color map can keep its quantitative color scale because that color has a separate, explicitly labeled meaning.

For panel b, create a clear enlargement relationship. A small, low-contrast structural locator may show where the geometric example sits; a larger, clean local view should show N, Cα and C, the frame origin and axes, and only the points and displacement needed for the verified fixed-frame operation. If two residue frames are necessary, give them equal-sized, nonoverlapping insets and one clear relation between them. Remove the three arbitrary residue names and multiple global-axis overlays. Keep the local-to-global transformation formula only if it sits beside the geometry it explains. Its notation must follow the model audit; this review does not prescribe an alternative frame convention. A few carefully placed backbone atoms and geometrical relations will explain more than a whole protein under several large triads.

For panel a, let the biological input, shared geometry-aware encoder and two output quantities define the primary rhythm. Reduce intermediate headings to one text size, use a restrained background or an understated outline to group each stage, and use a single, consistent arrow style. A stack marked ×4 can express repetition without four almost identical large rounded outlines. The head can remain an accurately ordered miniature layer stack, but it should be subordinate to the encoder and outputs; every LayerNorm/Linear/SiLU strip need not receive its own dominant headline. Keep the meaning of μ and log σ² and the joint prediction intact. Avoid shifting an implementation detail into the graphic's main visual focus merely because it is easy to draw as a box.

For panels c and d, align their internal flow and visual weights with panel b. The attention-score ingredients can use tiny pictorial representations plus short labels, feeding one clearly defined score/weight operation, rather than three large text-only cards. Scalar, pair and point colors must match panel a. The MD supervision panel should show the trajectory-to-label-to-loss connection clearly; trajectory snapshots should be aligned and comparable in scale. Displaying training loss and inference outputs must not imply MD is required at prediction time. Do not add decorative protein copies, grids or atom diagrams if they do not carry a model-specific concept.

At the intended printed width, target approximately 8–9 pt for normal annotations, 9–10 pt for stage titles and 10–11 pt for panel letters, with a single sans-serif family. These are design recommendations for this artifact, not claimed Genome Biology requirements. Use normal weight for routine labels, bold only for the hierarchy needed to navigate the figure. Keep line weights, arrowheads, corner radii, gaps and alignment uniform. The final test is not how detailed the canvas looks at monitor zoom: at actual manuscript width, the reader should identify the biological input/output immediately, follow the main path without crossing arrows, and understand panel b without tracing labels through a ribbon structure. Export an editable vector source together with PDF and a high-resolution PNG; inspect both the standalone figure and its inserted manuscript page.
