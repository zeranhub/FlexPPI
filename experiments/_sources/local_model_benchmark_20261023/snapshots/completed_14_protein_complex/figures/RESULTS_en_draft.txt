Local completed-cohort comparison of proteins and protein complexes.

Fourteen method/configuration entries were evaluated on identical frozen valid residues across 3,063 records (961,321 RMSF positions), comprising 2,203 proteins and 860 protein complexes. Protein identity was assigned from sequence-matched official molecular annotations before binary-group performance aggregation. Homomultimers and split domains of the same mature protein were classified as proteins; assemblies containing different proteins or independent heterologous peptide partners were classified as protein complexes. All 244 TCR–pMHC records are included in the latter group. Existing predictions, targets, masks and per-record scores were retained unchanged.

For proteins, FlexPPI achieved mean Spearman correlation 0.791, high-RMSF average precision 0.738 and mean absolute error 0.685 Angstroms. Corresponding chain-wise BackFlip-2.1 results were 0.898, 0.851 and 0.409 Angstroms. The paired Spearman difference (FlexPPI minus chain-wise BackFlip-2.1) was -0.107 (95% family-cluster bootstrap interval -0.118 to -0.094). The highest external mean Spearman correlation in this group was 0.898 from backflip-2.1 (chains). Full results for all completed entries and metrics are provided without an aggregate ranking score.

For protein complexes, FlexPPI achieved mean Spearman correlation 0.809, high-RMSF average precision 0.711 and mean absolute error 0.300 Angstroms. Corresponding chain-wise BackFlip-2.1 results were 0.720, 0.510 and 0.865 Angstroms. The paired Spearman difference (FlexPPI minus chain-wise BackFlip-2.1) was 0.089 (95% family-cluster bootstrap interval -0.025 to 0.138). The highest external mean Spearman correlation in this group was 0.763 from backflip-1.0-seq (chains). Full results for all completed entries and metrics are provided without an aggregate ranking score.

These results compare released weights and frozen out-of-fold predictions under a common reference, with unequal and partly unresolved training exposure. Full/Intra exclude each evaluated record and its biological group from the corresponding fold, but 904 evaluated records have related-family members in training. The 244 TCR–pMHC records form one dependency family within the protein-complex group, so these results do not establish generalization to unseen sequence families. Intervals resample families while retaining record-weighted means. Relative flexibility scores are not calibrated to Angstroms or substituted for the joint side-chain state entropy Hχ. Full-cohort PLANET and RMSF-net runs remain deferred.
